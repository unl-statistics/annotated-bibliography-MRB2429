Reverse Outline – Models and Rating Systems for Head-to-Head Competition

Author

Max Buettenback

Overall Purpose

  • Reviews statistical models that are used to help evaluate and rank competitors in head-to-head competition.
  • Explains how rating systems were developed from foundational probability models.
  • Discusses applications of these rating systems and also areas for future research.

Introduction

  • Explains the importance of ranking competitors in competitve settings.
  • Discusses the challenges in accurately measuring competitor skill.
  • Provides different historical contex for how rating system was developed.
  • Outlines the structure and goals of the article.

Paired Comparison Models

Bradley-Terry Model

  • Introduces probability-based model for comparing competitor strengths.
  • Defines how winning probability relates to skill parameters.
  • Describes interpretation of model parameters.

Thurstone-Mosteller Model

  • Presents alternative paired comparison model.
  • Describes statistical differences compared to the Bradley-Terry model.
  • Advances the understanding of how competition can be modeled.

Model Estimation and Properties

  • Explains estimation techniques.
  • Discusses different assumptions and limitations.
  • Shows how rankings are generated.

Dynamic Skill Models

  • Extends earlier modeling approaches.

State-Space Modeling

Applications, Limitations, Future Directions

Real-World Uses

  • Sports ranking systems.
  • Esports and gaming.
  • Tournament seedings and also predictions.

Limitations

  • Guessing how model works.
  • Data imbalance issues.
  • Getting model to guess right all the time.

Future Work

  • Suggests more development of more flexible models.
  • Encourages improvement in efficiency.
  • Identifies need for broader application and evaluation.

Gaps or Missing Areas
